## Charges that stuck

1. The plan gives up the paycheck first and risks savings worth only five months of rent (3 jurors).
2. About 13 sales a month are far short of the roughly 57 needed to cover rent (2 jurors).
3. Nothing says when to stop before the savings are gone (2 jurors).

## What the defense got right

1. People paid: 40 sales at $28 in three months of spare-time work (who bought is unrecorded).
2. The plan is unproven, not doomed: the record cannot say whether hours or demand limit sales, so flat sales are only one outcome.

## Conditions for acquittal

Do these this week; each answers one juror:

1. Statistician: list sales month by month; they must be rising fast enough to reach about 57 a month within five months.
2. Parent: ask your employer in writing for an approved leave or a promised re-hire, so failure costs the savings but not nursing income for good.
3. Lawyer: ask your landlord for written consent to run the business from home, and buy product-liability insurance for it.

## The sentence

My order: do not hand in your notice yet. Keep the paycheck while you gather these three items; a granted leave would also let you test full-time hours with a way back. Before resigning, write down the date and sales level at which you stop. Then bring the plan back.
