# Prosecution: opening

As written, the plan quits a nurse's income on the strength of about $373 a month in gross sales.

**1. Sales cover under a quarter of rent.**
Evidence: "40 sales in the last 3 months, at $28 each" is $1,120 gross, about $373 a month. "Their rent is $1,600 a month."
Cost: at today's pace, at least $1,227 a month drawn from savings on rent alone. Covering rent needs 58 sales a month, more than four times today's 13.

**2. At today's sales the savings last under seven months.**
Evidence: "They have $8,000 saved." That is five months of rent with no income; counting gross sales as pure profit stretches it to about 6.5. Both count rent as the only cost. The record lists no materials, Etsy fees, shipping, food, utilities or insurance, and no other income to offset them.
Cost: the whole $8,000 gone in under seven months, with the job already given up.

**3. Nothing shows spare-time sales scale to full time.**
Evidence: "They make the candles at home in their spare time today." The record gives one three-month total, with no monthly breakdown, hours worked, traffic or repeat buyers. It cannot tell growth from decline, or hours from demand as the limit on sales. If demand is the limit, more hours make more stock, not more sales.
Cost: sales stay near $373 a month while the paycheck stops.

**4. The plan spends the job first and tests demand second.**
Evidence: the only plan on file is "Quit my job as a nurse to sell handmade candles on Etsy full time." It names no income target, sales milestone, stop point or reduced-hours step, and no nurse salary, so what is given up is never priced.
Cost: the $8,000 is both the test budget and the safety net, and nothing says stop before it is spent.
