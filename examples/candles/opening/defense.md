# Defense: go ahead as described

The plan is a bet on a product that already sells, with five months of rent in savings.

Conceded: about $373 a month in gross sales ($1,120 over three months) covers under a quarter of the $1,600 rent, so today's income cannot carry the plan.

1. **Demand is proven in money.** Evidence: "40 sales in the last 3 months, at $28 each". Objection: that is small. Answer: the objection concerns volume; the evidence concerns whether anyone pays, and they did, for candles made in spare time. Low volume at a proven price is a better starting point than an untested idea.

2. **The runway is measurable.** Evidence: "$8,000 saved" against "$1,600 a month" is five months of rent. Objection: rent is not the only cost. Answer: other costs will shorten it, but five months is longer than the three that produced the sales record, enough to see whether full-time hours lift today's roughly 13 sales a month.

3. **Hours are the variable the plan changes.** Evidence: candles are made "in their spare time today". Objection: demand, not time, may cap sales. Answer: the record does not say which; full-time hours are the direct test, and the runway pays for it.

4. **No new fixed cost.** Evidence: candles are made "at home". Objection: the bar is still $1,600 a month. Answer: that is the user's living cost, rent already owed, and the business adds no workshop lease on top.

Missing from the record, and it matters: who bought, where, and whether monthly sales are rising (decides if demand is real); cost per candle, Etsy fees and other living costs (decide the true runway); hours worked now and whether home production can scale (decide if full time helps); whether nursing work can be resumed (decides how bad failure is).
