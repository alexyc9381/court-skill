# Defense: rebuttal

**1. Survives as arithmetic, not as a verdict.** $373 a month is what spare-time work produced, and the point says nothing about the rate once the person works at it full time.

**2. Survives if sales stay flat.** The runway sums are right and unlisted costs only shorten it, but the record is silent on other income and on whether nursing work can be resumed, which decides how final the loss is.

**3. The gap survives; the cost does not.** The prosecution admits the record cannot tell growth from decline or hours from demand, so its cost line, "sales stay near $373 a month," states only one branch of that unknown.

**4. Survives in part.** The missing stop point, milestone and salary are a real gap in the plan as described, but 40 sales in three months is already a demand test at spare-time scale, so "tests demand second" overstates it.

**For the jury: point 3.** Points 1 and 2 are the flat-sales case; point 4 is a gap, not a failure. So decide whether today's rate is limited by hours or by demand, which the record cannot say. Missing: a monthly breakdown (trend), weekly hours (headroom), who bought (strength of the test). Does that silence mean the plan fails, or only that it is unproven?
